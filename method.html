Method

How the forecast is built

A forecaster fitted to 68 full MCMC analyses of simulated catalogues, scaled to the paper’s published errors at its own configuration.

1. The analysis being forecast

The paper fits the vertical distribution and kinematics of Hipparcos K giants with the Jeans–Poisson equations. Total density follows ρ(z) = ρ0 sech²(z/zρ), with stellar velocities described by a mixture of two isothermal components. Selection effects are handled with a Poisson point-process likelihood using the Hipparcos completeness law V ≤ 7.3 + 1.1 sin|b|, and missing radial velocities are marginalised exactly. Posteriors are sampled with an ensemble MCMC (emcee).

2. Simulated catalogues

Catalogues were drawn from the best-fitting model with the paper’s selection function, including Gaussian parallax errors applied before the distance selection, a shifted assumed completeness limit, and a variable fraction of stars with radial velocities. Each was analysed with the full pipeline. The 68 runs follow a space-filling (Sobol) design over four inputs, plus 8 repeats at the paper’s configuration:

InputSimulated range
Stars in sample800 to 12,000
Fraction with radial velocity30% to 100% requested
Parallax error σϖ0.0 to 2.5 mas
Assumed limiting-magnitude shift ΔV-0.5 to +0.5 mag

3. Statistical error

The posterior width σ(ρ0) was fitted with a log-linear model in the number of stars N, the radial-velocity fraction fRV and the parallax error σϖ:

ln σstat = -4.22 -0.48 ln(N/3585) -0.05 ln(fRV/0.6) -0.14 σϖ +0.07 σϖ² +0.28 ΔV²

The N exponent is close to the Poisson ½. The fitted surface reproduces the simulated widths with 8% rms scatter, shown as the shaded band on the forecaster.

4. Completeness systematic

An error σΔV on the limiting magnitude moves ρ0 by (dρ0/dΔV) σΔV. The slope is measured from the simulations as a function of parallax error and is nearly independent of N, so the systematic sets a floor that more stars cannot lower.

5. Parallax offset

Parallax errors scatter stars across the distance limit, and distances computed from noisy parallaxes shift the recovered ρ0. The forecaster reports this offset relative to the paper’s 0.8 mas and, unless corrected, adds it in quadrature to the total.

6. Total error

σtot = √(σstat² + σsys² + σplx²)

N* is the sample size at which σstat equals the combined floor. Sample sizes for a target precision come from solving σtot = target.

7. Scaling to the paper

The statistical and completeness terms are scaled so that the forecaster returns the paper’s published ±0.016 (statistical) and ±0.017 (completeness, σΔV = 0.3 mag) at the paper’s configuration: 3,585 stars, 59% with radial velocities, σϖ = 0.8 mas. Away from that point the dependence on each input comes from the simulations.

8. Scope

  • The forecast applies to this analysis: K giants, vertical Jeans equilibrium, a two-component mixture, Hipparcos-like completeness. It is not a forecast for other tracers, other methods or other surveys.
  • Simulated catalogues follow the fitted model, so departures from equilibrium and from the mixture assumption are not in the budget.
  • Regions of the chart outside the simulated range are extrapolations and are shaded.
  • The simulated central value of ρ0 scatters about the input by roughly one posterior width, so a forecast describes the size of the error bar, not where one particular measurement will land.
