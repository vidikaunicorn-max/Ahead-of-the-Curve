Error budget vs. sample size

Based on a research paper. This tool implements the error budget of “Local mass density from Hipparcos K giants: a vertical Jeans–Poisson analysis with a mixture of isothermal components” by Vidika Mirpuri, in which ρ0 = 0.063 ± 0.016 (stat) ± 0.017 (completeness sys.) M☉ pc−3 was obtained from 3,585 stars. Forecasts are calibrated on mock catalogues generated and fitted with that paper’s pipeline.

Why this is useful

Statistical error shrinks as more stars are added, but the systematic error from not knowing exactly where the survey stops being complete does not. The two curves cross at a sample size N*. Beyond N* the limiting factor is calibration, not volume, so a larger observing allocation buys almost nothing.

Use the tool to size a spectroscopic follow-up, to decide whether to spend time on radial velocities or on photometric calibration, or to teach how error budgets behave in astronomy. See the method page for the model, calibration and limits of validity.
